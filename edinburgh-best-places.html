Best places to visit in Edinburgh:
Fewer stops, longer looks.

Five places that reward your time, and an honest guide to visiting each one without turning your trip into a race.

By Elsewhere Places · Walking · Slow travel

In this journal

First impressions Edinburgh Castle National Museum of Scotland Calton Hill Holyrood Park A quiet reading room Two unhurried days Before you go

First impressions: choose a few and go deep

Edinburgh packs a great deal into a small centre, which makes it easy to overplan. A long list of landmarks leaves little time to enjoy any of them.

Our picks are deliberately few. Each is a place where you can spend a real hour or two, and each has a quieter alternative if the crowds or the weather are against you.

Opening hours, ticket rules and access change often, so treat this guide as inspiration and confirm the details on each official website before you travel.

Edinburgh Castle: plan it, then give it time

Edinburgh Castle stands on an extinct volcano above the Old Town and forms part of the city's World Heritage Site. It is a national symbol, and it has long been the home of the Honours of Scotland, the country's crown jewels. [1]

It is also one of the busiest places in the city. Book your tickets through the official website, choose an early slot if you can, and check which displays and areas are open on the day, because some exhibits close for refurbishment or move.

Allow at least two hours inside, then leave the rest of the afternoon free. Do not queue for every room. Pick the parts that interest you, such as the views from the walls or the oldest buildings, and enjoy those properly.

Choose fewer stops. Give each one more attention.

The National Museum of Scotland: a free, rainy-day favourite

The National Museum of Scotland is on Chambers Street, close to the Royal Mile. Entry to the permanent galleries is free and it is open daily, apart from Christmas. [2]

There is more here than you can see in one visit, so pick two or three galleries and leave the rest for another day. A few special exhibitions charge, and the museum has a café and a brasserie if you need a break. [3]

Finish on the rooftop terrace, which gives a wide view over the city. It is a good place to sit for ten minutes and work out where you have been. [3]

The slower alternative

If the main halls feel crowded, visit one gallery only, then walk to a nearby café. A short, focused visit is better than a tiring marathon.

Calton Hill: the easy viewpoint

Calton Hill rises close to the centre and offers wide views over the Old and New Towns and out towards the Firth of Forth. [4]

The hill is free to visit, and its landmarks include the unfinished National Monument, the Nelson Monument and the old City Observatory. Climbing the Nelson Monument costs extra, so decide on the day whether it is worth it for you. [5]

Go in the early evening, bring a layer, and stay until the light changes. Take care on the paths after dark and keep to the lit routes.

Holyrood Park: wild ground in the middle of a city

Holyrood Park is managed by Historic Environment Scotland and is free to enter. Its centrepiece, Arthur's Seat, is an extinct volcano and the highest point in Edinburgh, at around 251 metres. [6]

You do not need to reach the summit. Walk part of the way, find a rock to sit on, and enjoy the view across the city. Choose a route that suits your fitness, footwear and the weather, and follow park signs, because some paths can be closed for safety.

The park's rangers run guided walks, which are a good way to learn about its geology and history. Check the official website for current times. [6]

A quiet reading room

Every list of Edinburgh sights is full of towers and hills. Add one indoor place that asks for nothing but your attention. The city's grand reading rooms are free or low-cost, work in any weather, and show a side of Edinburgh that most visitors miss.

Read our guide to Edinburgh's libraries for three that are close together.

A suggested two-day plan

Use this as a loose frame, not a timetable. Swap the days around to suit the weather and your bookings.

Day one — The Old Town

Visit the castle in the morning with a booked ticket, then spend the afternoon at the National Museum of Scotland. Finish with a slow walk along the Royal Mile and an early dinner.

Day two — Views and open air

Take a gentle walk in Holyrood Park in the morning, then rest at a café. Return to Calton Hill in the early evening for the view, and leave time for dinner nearby.

Before you go

  • Book the castle ahead. Use the official website and arrive on time for your slot.
  • Check opening hours. Hours, ticket rules and access change, so confirm before you set out.
  • Pack for the weather. Edinburgh's weather can change quickly, so bring layers, a waterproof and comfortable shoes.
  • Keep outdoor plans flexible. Have an indoor alternative ready for wet or windy days.
  • Travel considerately. Stay on marked paths, take litter with you, and avoid blocking narrow routes for photographs.
